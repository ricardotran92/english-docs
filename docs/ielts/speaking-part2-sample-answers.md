## ⭐ **BÀI MẪU 1 – Chủ đề cực khó:**

### **“Describe a piece of clothing you dislike.”**

**(Mẫu 2 phút, Band 8– Native-like)**

I’d like to talk about a type of clothing that I really don’t like wearing, which is formal suits—especially the full set with a thick blazer and a fitted shirt. I know they look professional, but I personally find them uncomfortable and quite restrictive. Whenever I have to attend a formal event, I feel like I can’t move naturally, and the whole outfit makes me feel too stiff.

What’s interesting is that this discomfort actually made me think about how clothing affects our behavior and even our productivity. Because I study AI and human–computer interaction to some extent, I often look at everyday things through a more technical lens.

For instance, in some computer vision datasets I worked with, clothing can significantly influence detection accuracy. People wearing bulky clothes, like suits or jackets, can confuse algorithms that estimate body posture or detect movement. Even in cybersecurity applications—such as surveillance systems—clothing introduces noise that models must learn to handle. When I was researching deep learning for image processing, I noticed that many datasets needed extra preprocessing because clothing style caused too much variance.

So while I dislike wearing suits for personal comfort reasons, it also reminds me of how seemingly simple things—like fabric, color, or shape—can impact AI models. It shows why machine learning requires powerful pattern-recognition capabilities and good generalization.

Overall, suits look nice on other people, but for me, they’re uncomfortable, impractical, and they always make me think about the challenges they create in image-processing tasks.

## ⭐ **BÀI MẪU 2 – Chủ đề siêu khó đỡ:**

### **“Describe a plant grown in your country.”**

**(Mẫu 2 phút, Band 8–8.5)**

A plant that’s very common in my country is the lotus. It grows in ponds and lakes, and many Vietnamese people consider it a symbol of purity because it rises beautifully from muddy water. I’ve always found lotus flowers elegant, and they appear a lot in traditional art, food, and even architecture.

What’s surprising is that, although it’s just a plant, it actually reminds me of some concepts in AI—mainly because of how resilient it is. As someone who works with machine learning and cybersecurity, I often look at things in terms of systems adapting to challenges. The lotus grows in harsh conditions yet remains stable, just like how robust AI models must perform well even when the input data is noisy or unpredictable.

In fact, when I was working on my research about detecting malicious PDF files using machine learning, robustness was one of the core issues. Attackers constantly modify files to evade detection, similar to how an environment changes around a plant. If a model isn’t designed carefully, it fails under slight perturbations—similar to a weak plant dying from small changes in the environment.

Even in deep learning, we have the concept of **adversarial attacks**, where tiny changes fool the model. The lotus, in a way, symbolizes what we want: a system that maintains its integrity regardless of the noise around it.

So although it’s just a traditional plant, I find it meaningful. Not only does it represent Vietnamese culture, but it also reminds me of the importance of resilience and stability—values that are central to AI model design as well as cybersecurity defense.

### ⭐ **1. Describe a smell you dislike**

A smell I really dislike is the odor of burning plastic. It’s sharp, toxic, and very uncomfortable. Whenever I accidentally smell it, I immediately feel uneasy because it reminds me of pollution and unsafe environments.
Interestingly, because I study AI and cybersecurity, I often think of safety in a broader sense. This smell makes me think about digital “pollution” — things like malware, phishing files, or corrupted datasets. In my research on malicious PDF detection, I analyzed thousands of harmful files, and they felt like the “toxic smells” of the digital world.
So even a simple unpleasant smell reminds me why building secure AI systems matters.

---

### ⭐ **2. Describe a type of weather you dislike**

I really dislike extremely humid weather. It makes everything sticky and uncomfortable. Even walking outside feels draining.
From a technical perspective, it reminds me of unstable environments in AI models — situations where too much “noise” affects performance. When I was working on image-processing research, humidity sometimes affected image quality during data collection, making training harder.
So this kind of weather teaches me the same lesson as machine learning: you need stability to perform well.

### ⭐ **3. Describe an old object in your home**

An old item in my house is a vintage mechanical clock passed down from my grandparents. It’s a bit worn but still functional.
As someone who studies AI, the clock’s internal gears remind me of model architecture design. Each gear plays a role, just like each neural network layer contributes to performance.
It shows how even old designs can inspire modern systems.

The Temple of Literature in Hanoi is a historical building I really admire. It represents education, discipline, and generations of scholars.
Because I aim to become a lecturer and researcher, this place resonates with me. It reminds me of how knowledge has evolved — from classical learning to today’s AI-powered research.
To me, the building symbolizes lifelong learning.
